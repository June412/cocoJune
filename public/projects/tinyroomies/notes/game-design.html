Care

Each interaction identifies whose need is involved, what the player can do, and how the resident responds. Habits and personal boundaries recur across several activities.

Continuity

The same furniture moves from dismantled parts through restoration to daily use. Parts, material progress, decoration and placed objects remain connected by stable identity.

Embodied Cooperation

Miniature reach, support surfaces, hands, tools and prop ownership shape the action. Resident assistance and one-hand fixtures organize work into understandable steps.

Story & Player Journey

The player’s commitment develops from a practical favor to an invitation. Early dialogue allows surprise or reluctance. Rebuilding a first bench does not presume an immediate emotional bond: the player learns about the move through later furniture, shared routines and the residents’ belongings.

  1. Preparation

    Choose language, preferred hand, captions, sound and one-hand assistance. Read the Quest room data and establish the workbench support.

  2. Discovery

    Narration introduces the purchase. The suitcase appears and shakes. The player opens the latches; the residents emerge and explore its lid.

  3. First Agreement

    Recall the residents, finish introductions and respond. Unfold the workshop and start the bench.

  4. Restoration & Use

    Complete each furniture recipe, place the piece and enter the activities or exchanges it enables. Earlier objects stay available.

  5. Invitation

    With all six core pieces placed, gather at the shared table and accept the residents’ stay. Place Page’s belongings and the nameplate, return tools and close the workbench.

  6. Shared Home

    Story completion enables the four extended projects and tea. The player can continue making, rearrange furniture and revisit daily life.

Recipe Scope

There are 131 configured assembly operations across ten projects. A subassembly step can contain several mesh parts. The teaching collection separately contains 16 lessons and 284 part records, including six joint foundations.

Availability

Brush modes and pigment mixing belong to the active recipe. The record player and Sprout’s stool use the flat brush without mixing. Only the shared-table recipe adds wax after paint. Extended furniture becomes available after the settling-in story completes.

Assembly & Tools

Part Sequence

A component must belong to the current furniture and satisfy the authored dependencies. The matching part aligns when brought close to its connection. Ordinary handling uses grip to carry and release to seat; one-hand assistance uses a grip toggle and a subsequent press to confirm.

Seating and fastening remain separate. A placed component can remain pending while a mallet or screwdriver action secures it. Dependent steps open after the required fastening. Loose parts can rest on valid supports, including fixed wooden beams, and can be picked up again.

Instruction Data

Stable part IDs, final transforms and subassembly groups connect the recipe to the production mesh. The manual presents the finished object, exploded sequence, transparent context and current joint. Highlighting and part numbers identify the same part in the manual and the workshop.

Assembly order preserves support and access: legs and rails precede closing frames, panels or tops. Doors and folding components carry explicit pivots and fastening points.

Valid Contact

The current tool, working region, movement and tracking state determine progress. A stationary tool, the wrong tool, contact outside the target or lost tracking leaves work unchanged.

Continuous Feedback

Wood fibers, roughness, debris, cut grooves, pegs and tool sound follow the active operation. The same workpiece remains present as the recipe moves from assembly through finishing.

One-Hand Handoff

A clamp or stand takes over support at the chosen position. The player releases, changes grip and performs the next operation with the same hand. The fixture owns support; the player still owns positioning and tool work.

Pattern & Relief

Press and rub the pattern sheet against the wood to transfer the design. The first transfer movement locks one of two variants. Outline work establishes the contour; shallow relief develops the authored region. Fine sanding and brushing prepare it for paint.

The carving mesh is a shallow height field with 5 mm backing and a 2.4 mm relief range. Regional progress changes the visible geometry. The authored motif defines the working regions and completion checks.

Palette & Strokes

Choose Sunny, Woodland or Dusk before the first motif dab locks the palette. Take the designated P-numbered pigment and use the available brush mode. Trigger-controlled strokes apply paint to the current surface; the required pigment and regional coverage determine completion.

Mixing is available in the configured recipes. Undo removes the latest stroke group. Stroke data stays local to the part, preserving the painted result through rotation, folding the workshop and reloading.

Material Rendering

Paint placement raycasts against the actual furniture mesh and samples stroke edges to avoid bridging empty spaces. Wood UVs carry grain through the mark. Bristle variation follows brush direction; fine-detail attenuation keeps the signal appropriate to screen footprint. Wet gloss decays over 24 seconds. Surface renewal follows completed work, with a three-second transition and reduced-motion handling.

Authored Performance

Action configuration chooses body motion and facial states such as Smile, Thoughtful and Shy. Resident-specific timing, gesture size, gaze and pauses preserve personality across speech and silence. Gait additionally responds to leg length, stride, turning and collisions.

Recorded Voice

The offline voice workflow uses original VoiceDesign descriptions, English phrase prosody, nonverbal processing and smoothing. The 228-recording library is routed by resident, intent and length. Both subtitle languages use the same sound bank.

Runtime Timing

The actual audio cursor samples the mouth envelope. The minimum performance hold is the longer of the authored duration and the clip length plus 0.12 seconds of lead allowance and 0.18 seconds of release allowance. Facial intent remains selected by the action configuration.

Care & Shared Memory

Cup Ownership

Sprout and Courier can lend their cups. Archivist first secures the bookmark. Keeper and Page retain ownership of their personal cups. Offering, actual handoff and agreement are independent conditions; reaching for a cup does not grant permission.

Response Completion

The system distinguishes a choice, its spoken response, the physical action and the resulting fact. Narrative memory records completed lines, choices and witnessed events. Object-specific states remain owned by the relevant life activity.

Page’s Sleeve

  1. Return Page’s empty cup upright to a stable support. Keep both hands free for the permission request.
  2. Complete the permission response before the sleeve becomes removable.
  3. Use a free hand to slide the sleeve through its seam, away from the handle. The cup stays supported.
  4. Rest the sleeve on its pad or return the same fabric along the entry direction.
  5. Reattachment resets the session permission. A later inspection begins with a new request.

Cup-mark inspection is a separate discovery. Its actual inspection and completed reply create the page-cup-mark-known fact. Sleeve removal alone does not create that narrative memory.

Keeper’s Fill Mark

Pouring above 77% of Keeper’s cup capacity starts his waterline incident. The player can let Keeper take a small sip or pour the excess into a serving cup. The chosen physical action and completed response establish the keeper-fill-mark memory; later dialogue recalls his preference.

Event Conditions

These incidents arise from current actions and objects. An event requires its configured participants, object identity and physical context. Each action branch checks its own result before the completed exchange is recorded.

Interruption

Pause stops the active presentation and timers. Prop-return handoffs finish before another activity takes ownership. Completed dialogue lines persist; an unfinished spoken response remains distinct from completed conversation.

Everyday Life

Music

Placing the record player opens its music activity. Six records and their sleeves provide physical selection. Disc transfer, needle position and playback share state with furniture-side controls; seek preview and seek confirmation are separate.

Shared listening requires actual playback above zero volume. Selecting a different track pauses the old record and begins a physical exchange: return the old disc to its sleeve, receive the selected disc, seat it on the platter and settle the needle before playback resumes.

Tea

Tea requires the shared table and completed settling-in story. Close the book and return the keepsake before deploying the tray. Prepare one of two flavors, add water, set down the thermos and close the pot lid.

Steeping requires at least 0.2 of normalized pot capacity, a closed lid and the thermos released and stationary. Eight active seconds reaches Ready; suspension pauses that counter. Actual utensil contact and tilt drive pouring into the selected cup. Serving and clearing preserve cup identity.

Reading

Packing and leaf journals are offered at the bench, reading chair, low bookshelf, shared table and planting/reading rack. The at-home journal also requires story completion. Each of the three books contains four spreads.

Opening, turning, closing and returning operate on one book instance. The right hand supports it while the left fingers release during page turns. A bookmark preserves position; narrative witnessing records a different fact.

Belongings & Plants

A photograph, packing label and timber sample appear through the sliding-lid box, display cabinet and shared table. Leaf observation belongs to the low bookshelf, Sprout’s stool and planting/reading rack.

Furniture-specific actions include storage, display, plant attention and workdesk tools. The action owns its prop contact and completion result, then submits a completed story fact when the exchange calls for one.

Room, Placement & Input

Room Adapter

MRUK 205 reads the room containing the player, preferring V2 with V1 fallback. The adapter retains the Unity OpenXR rig and converts geometry in physical meters while accounting for workbench scale and rotation. Automatic room loading and World Lock stay under the project’s existing lifecycle ownership.

The surface map contains floor boundaries, horizontal supports, walls, interior walls and furniture volumes. Localization waiting, bounded retries, permission handling, timeout and return-to-app loading govern the query. A failed refresh retains a usable map; manual workbench support stays consistent.

Furniture Placement

The player holds the side grip near a completed piece and carries it with either hand. The furniture follows the actual hand pose. Horizontal stick input changes yaw; vertical input changes pitch; holding the stick click while moving sideways adds roll. Releasing the grip places it at a clear pose.

Placement checks scanned walls and objects, the workshop, other furniture and residents. A scanned support surface is not required. Valid placement saves position, yaw, pitch and roll; a blocked release returns the completed piece to the workbench.

Resident Travel

A resident retains a destination while attempting a supported route. A gap, height difference or blocked route produces a request for a lift. Grip picks up the resident through the existing input entry point.

A valid intermediate landing keeps the destination. The remaining walk must finish before the destination activity starts. An invalid landing leaves the resident held. Tracking loss restores the last supported position; one request is active at a time.

Input & Access

Quest and simulation feed the same operation path. The scene uses one active rig, camera, AudioListener and input owner. UI displays the current state and submits requests; the craft, life and story systems own progress.

Settings include preferred hand, one-hand assistance, reduced motion, subtitle and font choices, English / Chinese text, grouped volume and mono audio. Color cues are accompanied by part numbers, words, audio and haptics.

Save Transaction

Event-driven saves and an approximately 20-second autosave preserve stable progress. Writes use a temporary file, replace the current save and retain a previous copy. Loading checks structure and values and can restore that previous copy.

New Journey

The project owns its application identity and save slot. Starting a new journey archives the current one through its own entry point. Old project bindings, anchors and saves remain outside this journey.

Leaving & Returning

Activity and production pause when the app closes. Continue restores completed work and stable life state, then starts room acquisition again. There is no offline production, simulated absence behavior or income accumulation.

Asset Integration

Character source packages remain read-only. Derived eye meshes and project-specific prop contacts live in this project with their provenance. The production manual consumes the same geometry and transforms as assembly. The separate Sprout shoulder and weight study remains a Blender authoring study.

Voice assets are produced offline from original descriptions and then processed for nonverbal playback. Runtime selects recorded performances rather than invoking speech generation.
